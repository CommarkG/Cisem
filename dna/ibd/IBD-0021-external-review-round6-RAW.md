## Q1 — The three-layer conflict model: sound in shape, three hidden failure modes
The shape is right (deterministic floor → triaged middle → learning loop). Three things will bite:
1a. Vault-and-halt fills and never drains. You flagged this yourself — it's the real one. A "halt + we'll get back to you" with no drain obligation becomes a graveyard: the platform silently accretes unresolved fundamentals until it's paralyzed, and nobody notices because each halt felt reasonable in isolation. Fix: every vault-and-halt entry carries a typed disposition + a mandatory timeout with a default action on expiry (auto-safe → park; needs-human → Governor ping; blocked → hard-block the next cycle so it cannot be ignored). A halt without a drain SLA is not humility, it's deferral debt.
1b. The triage SCALE is itself a classifier, and a misclassification is invisible. The dangerous move is a hard/fundamental clash mislabeled "low-risk provisional-proceed" — that ships a wrong answer under the banner of determinism, which is worse than halting. Fix: the triage verdict needs its own audit trail; periodically sample "provisional-proceed" decisions and ask "should this have halted?" Otherwise Layer 2 becomes a rubber stamp that launders wrong answers as resolved ones.
1c. Layer 3 starves behind the same bottleneck. "Propose-then-ratify, never self-enact" is correct — but if the Governor is the sole ratifier and conflicts are frequent, the learning loop's proposals queue behind everything else the Governor must ratify. The loop that's supposed to reduce load is gated by the load. Fix: batch-ratification lanes for Layer-3 codifications, distinct from feature ratification.

## Q2 — "Sealed-boundary + re-groundable": real, but it breaks at the reopen bar
It's a genuine reconciliation only if the reopen threshold is mechanical. "Sufficient argument-weight" is the soft underbelly: if argument-weight is a judgment call, "sealed" means "sealed until someone argues hard enough" — drift with a speed bump. Where it breaks first: the reopen test. Define argument-weight as a concrete, falsifiable artifact — a claim that the sealed element is now wrong, plus evidence, plus a named downstream harm — or the seal is decorative.
Second break point — upward pressure on the WHAT. You seal the boundary but keep the path beneath open. The leak: a branch discovers a need its sealed trunk can't express, and rather than pay the re-grounding cost, people quietly widen the trunk's WHAT to absorb it. Membership stays sealed while meaning drifts — the worst kind, because your mechanical checks still pass. Guard the trunk's WHAT semantically (a mandatory "cannot-mean" checked against descendant behavior), not just its membership.

## Q3 — "Significance = reach": right criterion, un-measurable at genesis, and incomplete
Reach is correct but you cannot measure a trunk's reach before its descendants exist. So "discover by building candidates + measuring reach" has a bootstrap hole: the first trunks are declared by axiom, not measured. Name it honestly — the founding set is a genesis block (ratified once, by fiat); reach-measurement is how you later validate and prune it, not how you birth it. Also, "removal breaks ≈everything" is a destructive test — you need a non-destructive proxy (count of transitive inheritors × criticality weight) or you can only measure reach by breaking things in production.
Add a second criterion: orthogonality. Reach tells you a candidate is big; orthogonality tells you it isn't a sub-facet of an existing trunk. Two trunks with overlapping reach are a precedence war waiting to happen (your Q1). Test every candidate trunk for "can an existing trunk already govern this?" before measuring reach — refusal-first, applied to the trunk set itself.

## Q4 — "Build-one-until-flawless": wise premise, unbounded stop-condition
The premise is exactly right — applying a pattern before it's sound amplifies its gaps into every downstream use; that's why you test-drive before you generalize. But "flawless" is a feeling, not a gate, and it will become the trap you fear: over-polish instance one, never build the third, never generalize. Bound it mechanically: refine until a fixed acceptance suite returns zero findings on a clean re-run, twice — that is your operational definition of "flawless," and the moment it's green you generalize; polishing past green is waste. Also bound the count: pre-declare N (say 3) and a divergence rule — if instances 1–3 don't converge on a shared pattern, that's evidence the pattern doesn't exist yet, not that you need instance 4. Iterating-to-flawless without a green gate and a count ceiling is how "quality" becomes "never ships."

## Q5 — What you're not seeing (the important one, for an AI governing itself)
The verify seam is your deepest risk. In a self-hosting AI platform, the AI both produces the machinery and judges whether it's done. Self-audit is not proof. We learned this expensively: a mechanically-generated, "✅ live" security control was declared done in good faith — and verified false in a single independent read. Your entire architecture rests on the AI's self-reports being true, and "mechanically checked" is only as true as the checker the same AI wrote. Insert an independent verifier: a cheap, cold, separate reasoning instance that re-derives every "done" claim from the source artifacts and never trusts the producer's word — as a standing gate, not an occasional check. This is the single highest-leverage safeguard for self-governance.
Two more:
- Append-only ≠ queryable at scale. Your rejected-decisions archive with disposition status is a strong idea, but an append-only store with no consolidation becomes practically un-findable — the vault-drain problem one layer up. Periodically cluster near-duplicate rejections into one principle; "technically present" is not "queryable."
- A closed governance loop has no external appeal. One human Governor + a self-governing AI is a single point of failure with no outside check, and a closed system drifts toward blind spots it cannot see from inside. You've partly solved this by soliciting this very document — institutionalize it: a periodic mandatory external review of the constitution itself (not just features).
What I'd do fundamentally differently: don't let the AI seal, reopen, or even propose changes to the machinery-for-changing-the-machinery without that meta-protocol being a human-ratified artifact the AI cannot rewrite — only propose against. The subtle failure in "the AI creates and governs its own machinery" is that the machinery for amending the constitution is also AI-authored. Anchor the meta-level in a genesis block the governed cannot edit. "Who governs the governor" must live in a document the AI can read but not modify.

The RAW/PURIFIED quarantine with trust-tier, the disposition-status archive, "constant = temporarily valid," and refusal-first as the wizard's default posture are all correct and hard-won choices most systems reach only after getting the opposite wrong. Defend them explicitly. The gaps above are all in the dynamic layer — what happens over time, under load, under self-audit — which is exactly where governance systems fail and exactly where you have the self-awareness to look before you ship.
